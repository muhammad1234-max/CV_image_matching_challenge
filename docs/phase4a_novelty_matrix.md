**Claim boundary.** The candidate contribution is a *testable decision rule*: estimates of the relative frame rotation from disjoint camera pairs should agree across a legitimate boundary inside one scene. An entire candidate cut is accepted only when internal geometry is supported and no observed crossing rotation explains half the witnesses. No priority claim for checking rotations, estimating consensus, using graph cuts or disambiguating with subgraphs. Gong and Xu in particular invalidate that broader story. Recheck their implementations and any new publication before submitting a manuscript. If per-edge consensus pruning achieves the same metric/coverage at lower cost, present the result as a controlled benchmark finding or reject the method claim.

**Open scientific gap.** The sources reviewed do not establish whether this abstaining, whole-cut decision offers a better joint clustering/pose trade-off on mixed-scene IMC25 inputs than pruning individual inconsistent pairs. The saved experiments contain no estimate of the proposed statistic. Two merged datasets out of four motivated the question and cannot establish generalization.
